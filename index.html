Category 1: Flag Registration Workflows
The Tuvalu Ship Registry is requesting specific statutory documentation for a newbuild (specifically Form S1, Declaration for ISM/DPA/CSO, and SMC/ISSC/MLC as per image_1d843f.png). Who is responsible for providing these, and how do we handle vessels operated by our counterpart, RVOS?

These specific items are strictly governed under the SOLAS ISM Code, ISPS Code, and MLC regulations. Because they dictate the long-term operational safety and security framework of the vessel, the delivery responsibilities are split as follows:

  • Primary Rule: These documents shall be provided by the appointed Ship's Manager. They are not within the newbuilding design engineering or shipyard production baseline scope.
  • Vessels Operated by RVOS: For any vessel slated to be operated by our counterpart, Rawabi Vallianz Offshore Services (RVOS), the Project Engineer (PE) must coordinate directly with the RVOS HSE (Health, Safety, and Environment) Department to obtain the paperwork.
Category 2: IMO Compliance & Core Numbers
What is the timeline and responsibility framework for securing an IMO number for a newbuilding contract, and how does flag registration change for 3rd party vessels?

Securing the vessel's unique IMO identity follows an uncompromising technical timeline linked strictly to commercial contracts:

  • Application Trigger: The Yard/Builder is legally responsible to apply for the IMO number immediately upon the Shipbuilding Contract (SBC) being signed.
  • Critical Timeline Point: The IMO number must be obtained before the keel is laid.
  • PE Action: The Project Engineer (PE) is responsible for proactively requesting this number from the yard and officially sharing it with both Class and Flag administrations.
  • Commercial Flag Assignment Matrix: An IMO number is mandatory to apply for flag registration (e.g., Tuvalu flag). Responsibility splits based on ownership:
    • RVOS Vessels: PE executes the application, but the actual financial payment is settled via the RVOS Account.
    • 3rd Party Vessels: Flag registration is the exclusive responsibility of the external Owner/Operator. It is entirely outside the scope of both VSE and RVOS.
Category 3: Hull Identification Basics
How must hull numbers be integrated into basic designer drawings for Class submission, and what is the protocol if a single series design of 10 vessels is split across 4 different shipyards?

The hull number is the unique identification footprint assigned by the builder/yard and is a critical prerequisite used to apply for the IMO number. PEs must enforce the following drawing control rules:

  • Title Block Control: The PE must ensure the yard hull number is permanently inserted into the basic designer drawing's title block prior to Class submission. Each individual drawing must capture at least one hull number.
  • Standard Series Design: If a series of vessels utilizes the exact same design, a drawing may list multiple hull numbers in the title block (e.g., one drawing listing all 10 hull numbers for a 10-vessel series built to identical specifications).
  • Multi-Yard Variance Split Strategy: When a 10-vessel series is split across 4 separate yards, drawing separation is governed entirely by equipment procurement types:
    • YFE (Yard-Furnished Equipment): If different yards source alternative equipment that alters foundations, weights, or layouts, these changes affect the basic design. The affected drawings must be split based on the specific yard's hull numbers.
    • OFE (Owner-Furnished Equipment): If the equipment is identical across all locations, the design remains completely unchanged. The same unified drawing must be used, listing all 10 hull numbers across all 4 yards.
Category 4: Class Notations & Equivalencies
What are the core engineering rules for selecting Class Notations during design and bidding phases, especially concerning major clients like Saudi Aramco versus external 3rd parties?

Class notations dictate the explicit structural and systemic design parameters of the build. PEs must navigate compliance according to the following client tier constraints:

  • The Compliance Chain: All chosen notations must follow a strict, unbroken progression:
    Client Tender Document → Request for Class (RFC) → Final Class Certificate.
  • Saudi Aramco Baseline (ABS Equivalency): Saudi Aramco baseline specifications typically mandate ABS class notations. When VSE introduces alternative classification societies (such as BV or RINA), the PE must actively request equivalent class notations to be offered.
  • Handling Technical Ambiguity: If any dispute arises regarding cross-society compliance, the PE must compel the proposing Class society to provide a formal written statement confirming that their proposed notation is fully equivalent to the client's requested ABS notation.
  • Non-Saudi Aramco Tenders (e.g., KJO, Taiwan Clients): Notations are dictated entirely by the localized client scope of work.
    The Minimum Notation Rule: PEs must offer the absolute minimum class notation configuration required to fulfill the technical tender. Do not append unsolicited or auxiliary notations, as they carry compounding design burdens and heavy associated class costs.

5. Phase 2 Production Knowledge Base: Aramco MCVS&R Standards

Category 5: Vessel Manuals & Deliverables
What are the baseline age, certification, and environmental constraints required when preparing statutory manuals and engineering packages for a Saudi Aramco contract assignment?
  • Vessel Age Calculation: Calculated strictly from the date the keel was first laid. It may only be calculated from delivery if the gap between keel laying and delivery is less than 2 years. Rebuild certificates are unaccepted.
  • Survey Milestones: Must complete a full dry-docking and UWILD before contract initiation, with at least 2 full years of validity remaining before the next required survey window.
  • Load Line Restrictions: Allowed to possess exactly one (1) valid load line certificate and one (1) load line marks set welded/embossed on the hull structure.
  • Conflict of Rules Resolution: If a conflict occurs between Classification Society Rules and the Saudi Aramco MCVS&R specification, the higher/more stringent engineering specification must prevail.
Category 6: Engineering Review Standards
What machinery performance specifications and ambient environmental ratings must the engineering team validate during design reviews for vessels operating in the Arabian Gulf?
  • Extreme Ambient Environment Baseline: All machinery/piping systems must guarantee continuous maximum operation without derating at:
    • Air Temperature: 45°C (113°F)
    • Relative Humidity: 95%
    • Sea Temperature: 34°C (93°F)
  • Speed & Powering: Main engine rating (BHP) is calculated at 100% MCR. Cruising service speed must be achieved at a maximum of 85% MCR while fully loaded down to the Summer Load Line draft in Beaufort 2 conditions.
  • Generator Redundancy: Minimum of two (2) main generators. If any single unit fails, the remaining generator must fully support all essential propulsion services and primary living habitability. Auto-start, parallel synchronization, and changeover functions are mandatory.
Category 6: Engineering Review Standards
What are the specialized design rules regarding Bow and Stern Thrusters for vessels built for Saudi Aramco tenders?
  • Watertight Isolation: All thruster assemblies must be completely housed within an independent, isolated watertight compartment.
  • Drive Train Constraints: Must be independently driven or electrically powered. They are strictly prohibited from being mechanically or hydraulically driven directly off the main propulsion engines or main generators.
  • Electrical Power Management: Electric setups must incorporate a robust power management system allowing all thrusters to operate at 100% capacity simultaneously alongside full shipboard operational load profiles.
  • Minimum Sizing Multipliers:
    • AHTS / PSV / DP Hulls / SP Jackups: 15% of main engine BHP with a minimum total force of 12 tonnes thrust.
    • Maintenance / Trash / Commissary Hulls: 15% of main engine BHP with a minimum total force of 6 tonnes thrust.
    • Other Classes: 15% of main engine BHP with a minimum total force of 4 tonnes thrust.
