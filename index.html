Production Knowledge Base

Category 1: Flag Registration Workflows
The Tuvalu Ship Registry is requesting specific statutory documentation for a newbuild (specifically Form S1, Declaration for ISM/DPA/CSO, and SMC/ISSC/MLC as per image_1d843f.png). Who is responsible for providing these, and how do we handle vessels operated by our counterpart, RVOS?

These documents are strictly governed under the SOLAS ISM Code, ISPS Code, and MLC regulations. Because they dictate the long-term operational safety and security framework of the vessel, the delivery responsibilities are split as follows:

  • Primary Rule: These documents shall be provided by the appointed Ship's Manager. They are not within the newbuilding design engineering or shipyard production baseline scope.
  • Vessels Operated by RVOS: For any vessel slated to be operated by our counterpart, Rawabi VSE Offshore Services (RVOS), the Project Engineer (PE) must coordinate directly with the RVOS HSE (Health, Safety, and Environment) Department to obtain the paperwork.
Category 2: IMO Compliance & Core Numbers
What is the correct technical timeline and responsibility framework for securing an IMO number for a newbuilding contract?

Securing the vessel's unique IMO identity follows an uncompromising technical timeline linked strictly to commercial milestones:

  • Application Trigger: The Yard/Builder is legally responsible to apply for the IMO number immediately upon the Shipbuilding Contract (SBC) being signed.
  • Critical Timeline Point: The IMO number must be obtained before the keel is laid.
  • PE Action: The Project Engineer (PE) is responsible for proactively requesting this number from the yard and officially sharing it with both Class and Flag administrations.
Category 1: Flag Registration Workflows
An IMO number is required to apply for flag registration (e.g., Tuvalu for an RVOS vessel). Who applies for this, who pays, and what is the rule if a vessel is built for a 3rd party?

The commercial flag assignment responsibilities vary completely based on project ownership:

  • RVOS Vessels: The Project Engineer (PE) is responsible for executing the application process, but the actual financial payment is settled via the RVOS Account.
  • 3rd Party Vessels: If a vessel is built for an external 3rd party, flag registration is the exclusive responsibility of that specific Owner/Operator. This process is entirely outside the scope of both VSE and RVOS. PEs must not accept flag-associated costs for 3rd party hulls.
Category 3: Hull Identification Basics
What is the purpose of the yard hull number, and how must the Project Engineer ensure it is reflected in design drawings for Class submission?

The hull number serves as the primary unique identifier from the builder and is a prerequisite for tracking class approvals and applying for the IMO number:

  • Mandatory Title Block Inclusion: The PE must ensure the unique yard hull number is permanently inserted into the basic designer drawing's title block prior to Class submission.
  • Minimum Threshold: Each individual engineering drawing must capture at least one valid hull number.
  • Standard Design Series: If a series of vessels utilizes the exact same design under a single project, a drawing title block may contain multiple hull numbers simultaneously. For example, a single design for 10 vessels in the same series can feature all 10 hull numbers on the drawing sheet.
Category 3: Hull Identification Basics
If a 10-vessel design series is split across 4 different shipyards, how does the procurement of Yard-Furnished Equipment (YFE) versus Owner-Furnished Equipment (OFE) affect the drawing title blocks?

When a single design series is distributed across multiple locations, drawing revision control depends entirely on equipment procurement boundaries:

  • YFE (Yard-Furnished Equipment) Impact: If individual yards source alternative equipment, components, weights, or foundations, these changes affect the basic design. The affected drawings must be split. Separate drawings must be issued featuring only the specific yard's hull numbers in the title blocks.
  • OFE (Owner-Furnished Equipment) Impact: If the owner provides identical equipment across all 4 yards, the engineering design remains completely unchanged. A single, shared drawing stays valid and must list all 10 hull numbers across all 4 yards.
Category 4: Class Notations & Equivalencies
How should Class Notations be selected during the bidding and basic design stage? What is the core compliance chain?

Class notations dictate the explicit structural, mechanical, and systemic design parameters of the build. They must strictly follow the client's specifications to avoid costly structural or system retrofits later. Every notation must pass through an unbroken compliance chain:
Client Tender Document → Request for Class (RFC) → Final Class Certificate.

Category 4: Class Notations & Equivalencies
Typically, Saudi Aramco will provide class notations based on the ABS Classification Society. What is the VSE protocol if we wish to use an alternative society like BV or RINA?

When executing projects destined for Saudi Aramco charters, ABS notations serve as the baseline specification. If VSE proposes alternative IACS members (such as BV or RINA), the following process is mandatory:

  • Equivalency Request: The PE must formally request equivalent class notations from the proposed society to be offered to the client.
  • Resolving Ambiguity: If there is any technical ambiguity or pushback regarding cross-society compliance, the PE must seek an official **statement of confirmation from the alternative Class society** proving that their proposed notation is fully equivalent to the requested ABS baseline.
Category 4: Class Notations & Equivalencies
What is the class notation selection strategy for non-Saudi Aramco tenders (e.g., KJO, Taiwan clients)?

For external non-Aramco tenders, notation profiles are subjected strictly to that localized client's technical requirements:

  • The Minimum Notation Rule: VSE engineering shall offer the **absolute minimum class notations required to fulfill the client’s exact tender specifications**.
  • Cost Control: Project Engineers are strictly prohibited from appending unsolicited or auxiliary class notations. Additional notations introduce more rigid design constraints, compounding survey requirements, and significant **associated certification and equipment costs**.
Category 5: Vessel Manuals & Deliverables
What are the baseline age, survey certification, and load line constraints required when validating designs and manuals for a Saudi Aramco charter?

Per the Saudi Aramco MCVS&R Chapter 1 rules, the following criteria must be completely met in the tech files and manuals:

  • Vessel Age Calculation: Age is calculated from the date the keel was first laid. It can be calculated from the delivery date *only* if the duration between keel laying and delivery is less than 2 years. Rebuild certificates are strictly unaccepted.
  • Survey Window Status: The vessel must complete a full dry-docking and an Underwater Inspection in Lieu of Dry-Docking (UWILD) before contract commencement, leaving a **minimum of two (2) years of validity remaining** before the next required survey window.
  • Load Line Cap: The hull is permitted to possess **only one (1) valid load line certificate** and exactly one (1) set of load line marks permanently embossed/welded on the hull.
  • Conflict of Rules: If any technical conflict occurs between the Classification Society Rules and the Saudi Aramco specifications, **the higher, more stringent specification shall prevail**.
Category 6: Engineering Review Standards
What extreme environmental baselines and main generator redundancy rules must VSE engineering enforce for Arabian Gulf operations?

All machinery calculations, heat exchanger surfaces, and load profiles must ensure continuous operations at Maximum Continuous Rating (MCR) without thermal derating under these strict conditions:

  • Ambient Environment Baseline: Air Temperature: **45°C (113°F)** | Relative Humidity: **95%** | Sea Temperature: **34°C (93°F)**.
  • Speed & Powering: Brake Horse Power (BHP) is evaluated at 100% MCR. Cruising service speed must be calculated at **85% MCR** while fully loaded to the Summer Load Line draft in Beaufort 2 sea conditions.
  • Generator Redundancy Rule: All hulls require a **minimum of two (2) main generators**. If any single generator fails, the remaining unit must fully support all essential propulsion services and primary living habitability loads. Emergency generators are separate and cannot be counted as part of this load-bearing calculation. Main units must have auto-start, parallel synchronization, and changeover features.
Category 6: Engineering Review Standards
What are the specialized compartment isolation and capacity sizing rules for bow and stern thrusters under Saudi Aramco specifications?

Thruster assemblies are subject to strict isolation and independent power management constraints under Section 4.5 of the MCVS&R:

  • Watertight Isolation: All thruster machinery must be fully housed in an independent, dedicated watertight compartment.
  • Drive Train Constraints: Thrusters must be independently driven or electrically powered. They **shall not** be mechanically or hydraulically driven directly off the main engines or main generator shafts.
  • Power Management: Electric setups must incorporate a Power Management System (PMS) that allows *all* thrusters to operate at 100% capacity simultaneously while all other shipboard equipment is running.
  • Minimum Sizing Multipliers: PEs must verify that total thrust output complies with the following main engine BHP ratios:
    • AHTS, PSV, DP Vessels & SP Jackups: **15% of Main Engine BHP**, with a minimum total of **12 tonnes** thrust.
    • Maintenance, Trash & Commissary Hulls: **15% of Main Engine BHP**, with a minimum total of **6 tonnes** thrust.
    • Other Standard Workboats: **15% of Main Engine BHP**, with a minimum total of **4 tonnes** thrust.
